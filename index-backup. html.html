The Journey Between Two Schools of Law

My education in law has been more than a journey between classrooms. It has been a journey through people, places, cultures, languages, challenges, and the many realities of Ethiopia.

01

Injibara University

Amhara Regional State • School of Law

My journey in higher education began at Injibara University, where I spent approximately three years studying law. Moving to Injibara was more than simply joining a university. It opened the door to a new environment, new communities, new languages, and new ways of understanding society.

During my time there, the journeys between university and home became part of my education. Along those roads, I encountered different people, communities, traditions, values, economic conditions, landscapes, and ways of life. I began to understand that the realities people experience outside the classroom are just as important as what we learn inside it.

Three years. One university. Countless lessons.
02

The Road Became a Classroom

The journey between Injibara and home became more than a physical distance. It became an opportunity to observe Ethiopia beyond the boundaries of a university campus.

I travelled through different parts of the country and encountered communities with different languages, cultures, norms, values, economic circumstances, and traditions. Every journey revealed another part of the country to me.

On my final journey back, I travelled through Benishangul-Gumuz and had the opportunity to visit and witness the area of the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD). I also travelled through very different landscapes, including dry and challenging environments, seeing places and communities that I had previously known only by name.

“Sometimes the road between two destinations teaches you more than the destination itself.”
03

Jimma University

Jimma • School of Law

My journey eventually brought me to Jimma University, where I continued my legal education. Jimma is especially meaningful to me because it is my birthplace.

Coming to Jimma was therefore not simply a transfer from one university to another. It was a return to my roots while continuing to move forward in my academic journey.

The contrast between my experiences at Injibara and Jimma has become an important part of who I am. One took me away from what was familiar and exposed me to new realities. The other brought me closer to my roots while giving me another platform from which to learn, serve, and grow.

A return to my roots. A continuation of the journey.
04

More Than Two Schools

Injibara University and Jimma University represent two important chapters of my academic life. But my education has never been limited to two campuses.

The roads I travelled, the people I encountered, the languages I heard, the cultures I experienced, the landscapes I witnessed, and the challenges I observed have all become part of my education.

These experiences have changed the way I understand law. I have learned that law is not only found in books, lecture halls, statutes, and courtrooms. Law lives among people.

It lives in their relationships, their communities, their rights, their struggles, their traditions, their economic circumstances, and their hopes for a better future.

Two Schools. One Journey. Many Lessons.

Two universities gave me classrooms. Ethiopia gave me the wider lesson.

Leadership & Service Journey

Long before university, I learned that leadership is not simply about holding a title. It is about earning people's trust, accepting responsibility, solving problems, and serving others.

From student council at high school to university leadership and community service, every responsibility has given me a new opportunity to learn, serve, and grow.

01

Student Council — High School

My leadership journey began at the high-school level, where I served in the student council. This was one of my earliest opportunities to represent students, work with others, and understand the responsibility that comes with people's trust.

02

Internal Inspection & Control

Oromia Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union — Ambuye

I worked in internal inspection and control at the Oromia Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union in Ambuye. This experience introduced me to organizational responsibility, accountability, internal control, and the practical realities of cooperative work.

03

Shanan Gibee Students Union

At Jimma University, I served first as an executive member of the Shanan Gibee Students Union and later was elected as its Secretary.

My experience in the union exposed me to student representation, coordination, organizational decision-making, communication, and the importance of working collectively to address students' concerns.

04

President — Charity & Volunteers Club

I have served as President of the Charity and Volunteers Club at Jimma University, where service became one of the central parts of my leadership journey.

Through volunteering and community-oriented activities, I have worked with students to turn the spirit of service into practical action.

05

Jimma University Law Students Association

Internal & External Affairs Director

I served as an executive member of the Jimma University Law Students Association as Director of Internal and External Affairs.

I also contributed to the editorial work of the JULSA Magazine — 3rd Edition, combining legal education, communication, writing, and student engagement.

06

Active Member — Student Organizations

My involvement has extended beyond one organization. I have actively participated in different clubs and associations, including:

  • Human Rights organizations
  • Anti-Corruption initiatives
  • GDASAO — Gumii Dagaagina Aadaa, Seenaa fi Afaan Oromoo
  • Other student clubs and associations
